Terms of use

Sheldon app (version 2.8.0). Effective from 25 October 2026.

What the app is

Sheldon is a free app for learning difficult and rare Polish words. Each word has a definition, an example of use and a link to its dictionary entry. No account is needed.

Word content

Most definitions were prepared with the help of artificial intelligence (AI), based on a Polish dictionary. Not all of them have been checked by a person yet — they may contain mistakes, and the app is not an official dictionary. For anything important (school, work, law), check the meaning in a dictionary — every word has a link. You can report a mistake from the word screen: “More → Report a mistake”.

All words and definitions are in Polish, even when the app itself runs in English or Ukrainian.

How the app chooses the order of words

The first word is always the word of the day — the same for every user.

The following words are drawn at random, but not completely at random. The app first picks a category, then a word from that category. A category’s chance depends on:

  • the number of words in the category — a new user gets a cross-section of the whole word base;
  • your favourites — the larger the share of your favourite words that come from a category, the more often its words appear (e.g. if you like psychology, you get more psychology words). When all your favourites are from one category, its words appear up to 4 times as often. The full effect starts at 5 favourites, and removing a word from favourites reduces it straight away;
  • how long you look at words — the app learns how long it usually takes you to read a word of a given length (your reading speed) and compares the time on each word with it. Lingering on a word longer than usual strongly raises the category’s chance, reading it calmly raises it a little, skimming it in a hurry lowers it a little, and scrolling past without reading (under 1.2 seconds) lowers it more. Over 2 minutes on one word is treated as a phone put down, not as reading;
  • what you do with a word — a like counts the most, and opening its details, listening to it and sharing it also raise the category’s chance; “Don’t show again” lowers it strongly. Older signals gradually count for less.

The same signals teach the app which difficulty level (I — easy, II — medium, III — hard) you like: within the chosen category, words of that level move to the front of the queue more often (at most 3 times as often). No level you have switched on disappears from the list.

To keep the list varied:

  • about 15% of words come from randomly chosen categories;
  • the same category never appears more than twice in a row;
  • words you haven’t seen yet come first, and within one pass through the base no word repeats.

All of this is calculated only on your phone. Data about what you read and like is not sent to the authors or to any server, and it is not used to choose ads.

You control the order yourself:

  • the “Categories” button on the Words tab, or “Settings → Topics and difficulty” — switched-off categories and difficulty levels never appear (you can choose, for example, only level I, or I and II together);
  • adding and removing favourites;
  • “Don’t show again” on a word;
  • “Settings → Your data → Delete all data” — this also erases the remembered interests, and the app starts choosing words from scratch.
